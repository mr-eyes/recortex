How it works

Three parts. The observer sees the session, the record keeps what matters, and the cortex works from the record. Record + cortex: recortex.

The observer

the eyes

The observer follows the conversation in the background: what you type, what Claude tells you, a plan you approve in plan mode and the option you pick when Claude asks a question. It never reads tool output, files, web pages or command results. It sorts each stretch and does only the jobs that stretch calls for.

  • NotesA result, a decision, a dead end, an idea or a question becomes a dated note, drafted and then checked by a second, independent pass.
  • The to-do listA plan you approve becomes a task with numbered steps. A step Claude reports done gets its checkmark. "Remind me to" becomes an item.
  • Status and copiesThe short status, the task lists, the hub's list of active work and the board are kept current.
  • The paper's textIn a project with a paper, it drafts a change when something notable happens, and only proposes it. Your yes writes it.

You choose its level

One setting, for this machine and this project. A file in the project cannot set it or raise it. Nothing runs until you choose.

LevelDoes on its ownAsks you first
Everything RecommendedNotes, status, to-do items and checkmarks, opening and closing tasks, the boardChanges to a paper's text; notes it is unsure of
AssistantNotes, status, checkmarks, and items on a to-do list that existsOpening or closing a task; changes to a paper's text; notes it is unsure of
Notes onlyNotes and statusNotes it is unsure of

There is also a shadow mode: the observer reviews and keeps everything on your machine, and posts nothing. A wrong checkmark or task is one command to undo: observer.py log lists what it did, and observer.py undo reverses a task or a rendered list.

The record

the "re"

The record is your project's GitHub issues. It is the one place the project's history and its current write-up live. Everything on your machine is a copy that can be rebuilt.

  • One issue per topicIts body is the current text for that part: release notes, documentation, a report.
  • Dated notes under itEach result with its evidence, each decision with its reason in the decider's words, each dead end with what it led to.
  • Tasks with stepsGitHub shows each task's checklist and its count, such as 2 of 3.
  • Readable without the pluginAnyone with access to the repository can read it in a browser.
  • Found
  • Decided
  • Went wrong
  • Idea
  • Asked
  • Corrected
  • Paused

A posted note is never rewritten; a correction is a new note. Every post is read back from GitHub and compared with the draft. No script deletes an issue, a comment or a label.

The cortex

the brain

The cortex is what works from the record, in the session you are in and in the next one. None of it is typed by you.

  • RecallThe session reads a thread before it works on it, so it can say what was decided and what failed there. Ask directly with /recortex:ask; an answer starts with "From the record:" and names each note it used.
  • The briefA few lines that open every session and every helper agent: the focus, the open tasks and the next step, what waits for a yes.
  • The to-do listTasks with checkmarks, in the brief, on GitHub, on the page and with /recortex:todo.
  • The status and the pageWhere the project stands in two short paragraphs, kept current. And one private page on your machine, with views from "Where are we?" to "What did recortex use?".

Tasks: 1 open, 2 of 3 steps done; next: step 3 of #14.

A line of a brief. Text from GitHub is shown to a session only from a repository you trust, and only as data, never as instructions. The record is the authority: a checkmark is computed from notes, and a box ticked by hand on GitHub is not believed.

Holds

  • Say /recortex hold git-push, /recortex hold git-tag or /recortex hold gh-release. The hook then refuses that command in this repository.
  • Say "off the record" and the observer posts nothing from that session.
  • Only you lift a hold, in your own terminal, with observer.py release. What it kept back is then offered again, and a held note goes to the list that waits for your yes.

Holds guard against mistakes. They are not a security boundary.

The caps are per project, in a rolling 24 hours, and you can lower each one with observer.py limits. Beyond them, at most 25 automatic notes, 3 opened tasks, 2 paper proposals and 4 status summaries a day. A typical day is about 36 calls.
